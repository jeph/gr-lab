# How GR Photo behaves

What GR Photo does on each screen and as the connection to the camera changes, in detail. The [README](../README.md) has the short version.

## The Cameras screen

The app opens on the Cameras screen. With a camera paired, the one used last is on a card with a chip that says how it is linked, a line drawing of the camera, a power button and large Library, Shoot and Looks buttons, and the app links to it over Bluetooth by itself if it is nearby when the app opens (unless Connect when nearby is turned off in Settings); other cameras are listed below it. While the camera is not linked the chip is a crossed-out Bluetooth sign with no words, and says Nearby once the phone hears the camera; it says Connecting... and its icon pulses while Bluetooth is being linked and turns green once it is; from the moment the camera's Wi-Fi is being joined it shows a Wi-Fi sign instead, pulsing until the Wi-Fi is up and green after, with the camera's battery. While Bluetooth is being linked, a × beside the chip cancels. It has a place of its own that is empty the rest of the time, and nothing else on the card moves or changes into another control as the connection changes, so a tap lands on what it was aimed at.

Tapping the chip, or the camera pill on the camera's pages, opens the camera sheet, which has a row each for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi saying what the link is doing, with Connect, Cancel or Disconnect. Connect on the Wi-Fi row joins the camera's Wi-Fi ahead of the Library. Disconnect on the Bluetooth row disconnects the camera altogether, its Wi-Fi included, and it stays disconnected until you connect it again or open one of its pages; Disconnect on the Wi-Fi row keeps Bluetooth. When connecting failed, the chip says so and the sheet says what to check, with Try again. Linking over Bluetooth never turns a camera on.

The power button turns the camera on or off over Bluetooth the way GR WORLD's does, linking it first when needed; a camera turned on with it stays on when GR Photo leaves it, and one turned off with it stays linked over Bluetooth. A camera that is off drops its Bluetooth link after a while by itself. GR Photo does not link it again and again then: the camera sheet says Not connected and the chip says Nearby while the phone still hears the camera (and nothing once it no longer does), GR Photo only listens for the camera saying it is on, and links it when it does. The chip says how the camera is linked and nothing else; whether it is on shows on the power button, which still links it and turns it on.

## The camera's pages and its Wi-Fi

The card's Library, Shoot and Looks buttons open the camera's pages on that page; a floating tab bar at the bottom of the pages switches between them. The pages slide in over the Cameras screen.

Unless you join it from the camera sheet, the camera's Wi-Fi is joined only for the Library and for live view, and opening either turns the camera on first if it is off. While joining, the page lists two steps, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi; if joining fails, it says why and what to check, with Try again. Joining takes a while, so once the Wi-Fi is up it stays up when you move to a page that does not need it or go back to the Cameras screen, whose chip then shows the Wi-Fi in green: the Library and live view open without joining again, and a download carries on. Auto-disconnect (below) or Disconnect on the camera sheet's Wi-Fi row leaves the Wi-Fi, with Bluetooth still linked. Going back while the Wi-Fi is still being joined stops the join. A camera GR Photo turned on for a page is turned back off when you go back from the camera's pages or, if its Wi-Fi stayed up, when the Wi-Fi is left on the Cameras screen.

## When a link is lost

If the Wi-Fi is lost while it is in use (the Library or live view is open, or a download, movie copy or playback is running), the page stays and the app gets it back by itself, shown as reconnecting; a camera switched off at its power button shows a red "Camera off" pill and is turned back on over Bluetooth. If the Wi-Fi does not come back, the app returns to the Cameras screen with Bluetooth still linked and the chip saying so; if the camera is gone with its Bluetooth too without having said it was switching off (out of range, or its battery removed), it returns there with the chip saying the connection was lost. A camera that said it was switching off is not gone, even when its Bluetooth goes off with it: the page says Camera off, and GR Photo links it and joins its Wi-Fi again when it is switched back on. If the Wi-Fi is lost while nothing uses it, GR Photo lets it go without a word instead, and a camera switched off then stays off. On Android the loss is noticed as soon as the phone reports it; elsewhere, when the camera stops answering a Library request or its liveness polling. One camera is connected at a time.

A Bluetooth link that drops is linked again by itself, shown as reconnecting on the camera's pages and by the pulsing icon on the card's chip. While only Bluetooth linked the camera, also when the Library or live view was opened meanwhile and is waiting for that link, one that is not back after 40 seconds counts as gone (out of range, or its battery removed): the app returns to the Cameras screen, whose chip says the connection was lost. The link of a camera that is off is the exception: it is not linked again and does not count as gone, as described under The Cameras screen, and opening Shoot, Looks or the Library links it and turns it on. The saved looks on the home dock stay in front when that happens while they are shown.

## Shoot

Shoot opens in the mode used last, Remote the first time. Remote works over Bluetooth alone: it turns the camera on in shooting mode (or switches a camera in playback to shooting), shows the mode dial, shots left and battery, and has a large shutter, the self-timer (off, 2 s, 10 s) and the drive mode. A running self-timer turns the shutter into a countdown ring; tapping it cancels. On Android the volume buttons fire the shutter too.

Live view joins the camera's Wi-Fi and shows the camera's picture, dark and without the tab bar: tapping the picture focuses there, the aperture, shutter, ISO and EV readouts open a dial of the values the camera offers in its current mode, and the chips change the drive, self-timer, metering and Image Control. The picture is shown whole and as large as the window allows, on phones, foldables and tablets alike; the controls go above and below it, and lie over its edges where the window leaves them no room of their own. In a window as short as a phone on its side they go in a rail on each side of the picture, which stays in the middle of the screen: ×, the camera and the chips on one side, the readouts, the shutter and the last shot on the other, with the dial along the bottom of the picture; × is then the way back to Remote. Switching back to Remote (or ×) keeps the camera on and its Wi-Fi up, so live view starts at once the next time; it only stops a join that is still under way.

The last shot, the square beside the shutter, is the newest picture taken since live view was opened, with the shutter in the app or on the camera, as the Library's grid shows it. Tapping it opens the viewer on the pictures taken since then, the newest first; live view stops under the viewer and starts again on the way back. Going back to Remote, or leaving the camera's pages, ends that set; the app leaving the front or the Wi-Fi dropping does not. A picture deleted on the camera leaves the set, also while the viewer is open or the app is not in front, as long as the Wi-Fi is up: the camera reports a delete only as it happens, so one made while the Wi-Fi was down is not noticed.

## Auto-disconnect

Auto-disconnect (Settings: 1, 5, 10 or 15 minutes, or Never; 5 by default) leaves the camera's Wi-Fi when GR Photo has not been used for that long, whichever screen is showing. With the Library or live view open it returns to the Cameras screen, the same way as disconnecting the Wi-Fi on the camera sheet; Shoot's Remote and Looks stay open on Bluetooth; and on the Cameras screen, whose chip says the Wi-Fi was left, a camera GR Photo turned on is turned back off. Touches, scrolls, key presses and moving between screens count as use, and so does a running download, movie copy, movie playback or live view. Time with the app in the background counts towards the limit. With Never, the Wi-Fi stays up until it is disconnected on the camera sheet, or the power button or switching cameras ends it.

On an iPhone the Wi-Fi, or a join still under way, is also left 20 seconds after GR Photo leaves the screen (another app in front, or the phone locked), whatever Auto-disconnect is set to, and sooner if iOS gives the app less time than that to run in the background. iOS stops a phone running GR Photo soon after it leaves the screen and may end it, and the camera network stays joined after that, with nothing left running to leave it or to turn the camera's Wi-Fi off. Back in GR Photo, the camera card's chip says the Wi-Fi was left in the background. The Wi-Fi stays only while iOS keeps GR Photo running for a download queue (iOS 26 and later, with the queue in a Live Activity); once the queue is done, or iOS stops it, the Wi-Fi is left before GR Photo lets iOS stop the app. A queue cut off this way waits for the camera, as after any other lost Wi-Fi. When GR Photo starts, it removes any camera network a run that iOS ended left joined. Android does not stop an app this way, and its join ends with the app, so there the Wi-Fi is left only by Auto-disconnect.

## Joining the camera's Wi-Fi

On Android, the first Wi-Fi join of each camera is explained before Android's own network list asks you to approve it. Android shows that list again for an approved camera after its network was disconnected by hand in Wi-Fi settings. When that happens, a short message names the network to choose, and if the join fails, GR Photo explains why the list came back and offers Try again.

Joining is timed around the camera's access point, which comes up a varying time after its Wi-Fi is turned on. When the camera is off, GR Photo turns its Wi-Fi on right after waking it instead of first waiting 8 seconds to see whether the wake drops the Bluetooth link. If that early write fails, it waits for the link as before; if it goes through and the join is still looking for the network 8 seconds after the wake, GR Photo turns the Wi-Fi on once more (linking again first if the link dropped). On Android, Android looks for the network when asked and then only every 10-20 seconds, so for an approved camera GR Photo asks again every 7 seconds until Android starts connecting. It does not ask again while Android's network list is open, or on phones that join camera networks on a second Wi-Fi connection.

## Geotagging

GR cameras have no GPS. GR Photo can add where a photo was taken in two ways, which are turned on separately for each camera and can both be on: live geotagging, where the phone sends its location to the camera and the camera saves it in the photos it takes, and background geotagging, where the phone keeps a history of where it has been and GR Photo adds the nearest recorded place to a photo when it is downloaded. A location the camera saved is never replaced. Both are off until they are turned on, and GR Photo does not ask for the phone's location before that.

Live geotagging is the Live geotagging switch on the camera's card, under the Library, Shoot and Looks buttons. The card is the current camera's, so another camera's switch is reached by making that camera the current one. The switch is not shown for a camera that cannot be sent a location. The line under its name says what it does and reads the same whether it is on or off. Turning it on goes straight to the phone's question about location, the first time. If the phone refuses (location not allowed, only approximate location allowed, or location turned off for the whole phone), live geotagging stays off and a second line under that one says which it is, with a button to the page of the phone's settings where it is allowed.

While live geotagging is on and the camera is on and linked over Bluetooth, GR Photo follows the phone's location and writes it to the camera: as soon as there is a position, again whenever the phone has moved (by more than 5 metres or the position's own accuracy, and not more often than every 5 seconds), and a newly taken position at least every 3 minutes while standing still. A position older than a minute or less accurate than 100 metres is not sent. The camera forgets a location 10 minutes after it last got one, so a photo never gets a location from long ago. Nothing is sent to a camera that is off, and the phone is not located for a camera that is off or away.

The camera only takes a location while its own Store Location Info setting is on. Turning live geotagging on in GR Photo turns Store Location Info on, and turning it off turns Store Location Info off, which also clears the location the camera holds; if the camera is off or away when the switch is flipped, this happens the next time it is on and linked. If Store Location Info is turned off in the camera's menu, GR Photo does not turn it back on by itself: a second line under the switch asks for Store Location Info to be enabled on the camera, with a Turn on button.

While it is on and nothing is in the way, the card says nothing more: that it is geotagging with the phone's location, or that the camera is not connected, shows only in the phone's notice of it (Android's notification, the Live Activity on iOS), which reads the same while the phone is still finding a position good enough to send as once the camera holds one. When the phone no longer allows the location (the permission was taken away, only approximate location is allowed, or location is off for the phone), live geotagging stays on, a second line under the switch says which it is, and a button opens the phone's settings; it carries on by itself once it is allowed again.

Live geotagging carries on while GR Photo is not on screen. The phone shows a notice while it does, named after the camera, with what it is doing and a Stop button: a notification on Android, and on iOS 17 and later a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. Stop turns live geotagging off for that camera, as its switch on the card does, Store Location Info on the camera included: it stays off, also when the camera is switched off and on again, until the switch is turned on again in GR Photo. That holds when Stop is pressed after GR Photo's window was closed, too: the app saves it before it ends. When the camera has not been on for an hour the notice goes away, and geotagging waits for the camera to be switched on again. iOS starts a Live Activity only for an app that is on screen, so when the camera is switched on after that hour with GR Photo still in the background, geotagging runs with no notice until GR Photo is next opened. A Live Activity the user swiped away, or that iOS ended after its eight hours, comes back the next time its text changes or GR Photo is opened; geotagging goes on meanwhile. Ending GR Photo in the app switcher ends geotagging and takes the Live Activity away. On Android, closing GR Photo's window while live geotagging runs closes the camera's pages and leaves the camera's Wi-Fi, as Disconnect on the Wi-Fi row does (a download still running keeps the Wi-Fi until it is done); Bluetooth and geotagging carry on.

Live geotagging starts once GR Photo is open and has linked the camera, and from then on whenever the camera is switched on while GR Photo is still running: in front, or in the background as described above. Nothing starts GR Photo for a camera that is switched on while the app is closed. When the camera comes on while GR Photo is in the background and the phone will not let it use the location from there, a second line under the switch says that geotagging starts when GR Photo is open, and it starts when GR Photo is next opened.

With several cameras paired, live geotagging runs for the current camera only: the one on the card, which is the one GR Photo links.

Sync camera clock, the second row of the This camera section in the camera's details (which ⋯ on the camera's card and the camera's row under Other cameras open), between the camera's name and the date it was paired, and on by default, keeps the camera's clock on the phone's time: when the camera is linked while it is on and every hour after that, GR Photo reads the camera's clock and sets it if it is 2 seconds or more off. It does not depend on live geotagging. While background geotagging is on for the camera the clock is kept in sync whatever the switch says, and the switch is shown on and cannot be changed; the line under its name, which reads the same at all times, says what it does and that it stays on while background geotagging is on: background geotagging goes by the time the camera wrote into each photo. A GR IV clears its location when its clock is changed, so GR Photo sends a newly taken position right after setting the clock.

Background geotagging is a switch for each camera, in the Location section of the camera's details. The line under its name says what it does and reads the same whether it is on or off. Learn more, the row below it, opens a screen that explains background geotagging: the phone keeps a history of its location for 90 days, also while GR Photo is closed, and a downloaded photo gets the phone's location from when it was taken; unlike live geotagging, GR Photo does not have to be kept active, and the location is slightly less accurate. The screen also says that the phone asks for location all the time when background geotagging is turned on, that the history is stored only on the phone and can be deleted in Settings, and that history older than 90 days is deleted, so photos downloaded more than 90 days after they were taken get no location; the camera's details carry none of that. Turning the switch on asks for location all the time, which background geotagging needs. iOS asks in an alert of its own, straight away. Android has no such question and opens GR Photo's location settings instead, so GR Photo first shows a dialog that says why, names the choice to make there as the phone words it ("Allow all the time" in English) and says that the location history is only used for geotagging photos and never shared, with Open settings and Not now; Not now leaves the switch off. Without location all the time the switch stays off and a row under it says what is missing, with a button to the phone's settings.

While it is on for at least one camera, the phone records where it is from time to time, also while GR Photo is closed, and GR Photo keeps those places for 90 days. There is one history for the phone, whichever cameras use it, and it stays on the phone. When a JPEG or DNG is downloaded from a camera that has background geotagging on and has no location yet, GR Photo writes into the downloaded file the place recorded closest in time to when the photo was taken, before or after; a photo taken before the first recorded place gets that place only if it was recorded within 10 minutes of the photo. The time is the one the camera wrote into the photo, which is one reason the camera's clock is kept in sync. Photos downloaded more than 90 days after they were taken get no location from background geotagging, movies get none, and the photo on the camera's card is not changed. While live geotagging runs and the history is being recorded, the positions it takes are kept in the history too.

If location all the time is taken away later, background geotagging stays on and a row under it says Paused, with what is missing and a button to the phone's settings, and resumes by itself when it is allowed again. Turning it off for the last camera that had it on stops the recording; what was recorded stays until it is 90 days old or deleted. Delete location history, under Location in Settings because the history is the phone's and not one camera's, asks first and then deletes every place recorded so far, including those the phone has not handed to GR Photo yet. Photos downloaded afterwards cannot get one of those places, and photos already downloaded keep theirs. Deleting does not turn background geotagging off for any camera: the phone goes on recording, so a photo taken shortly before the deletion can still get the next place recorded, if that is within 10 minutes of the photo.

## The map of where a photo was taken

In the viewer, which a shot in the Library opens and so does the last shot in live view, Info shows a shot's details below it. When GR Photo knows where a photo was taken, a map of the place is below the details, with a pin on the place. The place is the location the camera saved with the photo, which the camera had from live geotagging; for a RAW+JPEG pair it is enough that one of the two files has one. A photo without one gets the place background geotagging would give it at download, the one in the phone's location history recorded closest to the time the photo was taken, while background geotagging is on for that camera. That map is drawn from further out, the part of town rather than the streets, because the history is coarse. A photo with neither has no map and the viewer is as it is without one; a video never has a map.

So that the photo keeps the screen, only the top of the map shows under the details, enough to tell that it is a map and that there is more of it. Scrolling the viewer up brings the whole map into view. The map is a picture to look at: it is not dragged, zoomed or turned, so a drag over it scrolls the viewer. Info again, or a tap on the photo, puts the details and the map away. While a photo is zoomed the viewer does not scroll.

The map is drawn by MapLibre from OpenFreeMap's tiles, which are OpenStreetMap data, in the colours of the app's theme. The tiles are fetched over the internet, the only thing GR Photo fetches from anywhere but a camera (see the [privacy policy](../app/assets/privacy.md)). On Android, where everything else GR Photo sends goes to the camera's Wi-Fi while that is joined, the map's requests go over the phone's own internet connection instead, usually mobile data. Without an internet connection the pin stands on empty ground in the theme's colour.

## Foldable phones

On an iPhone Duo the camera's pages follow the phone's pose. Closed, and open and held wide, iOS keeps a strip down one side of the screen for bars that stand upright, with the time and the status at its top. GR Photo puts each page's buttons there, under the status (Back and Settings, New look on Looks, Info and Download in the viewer, × and the exposure mode in live view), and the tab bar stands upright at the foot of the strip with icons only. Open and held upright, the bars lie across the screen again and the top bar sits beside the status.

Where the screen folds down its middle with room for a page on either side, on an open iPhone Duo held wide or an Android foldable held the same way, the pages use the two sides:

- The Library has six columns. Tapping a photo shows it on the second page with its details, Download and the map, while the grid keeps the first; tapping that photo opens the full-screen viewer, and Back puts the second page away before it leaves the Library.
- Looks has the camera's Custom slots on the first page and the looks on the phone on the second. Holding a look and dragging it onto a slot loads it there, after the same confirmation as Load into from its menu.
- The look editor has the picture and the photos to preview on the first page and the controls on the second.
- In live view, open and held wide on an iPhone Duo, the picture has nothing over it: the camera and Remote / Live view go above it, the readouts and the chips below it, and the shutter and the last shot stand in the strip.

With the phone folded part of the way, nothing but a picture lies across the fold. Half-open on a table, live view and the look editor show the picture in the half that stands up and every control in the half that lies flat.
