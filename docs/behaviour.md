# How GR Photo behaves

What GR Photo does on each screen and as the connection to the camera changes, in detail. The [README](../README.md) has the short version.

## The Cameras screen

The app opens on the Cameras screen. With a camera paired, the one used last is on a card with a chip that says how it is linked, a line drawing of the camera, a power button and large Library, Shoot and Looks buttons, and the app links to it over Bluetooth by itself if it is nearby when the app opens (unless Connect when nearby is turned off in Settings); other cameras are listed below it. The chip says Not connected with a crossed-out Bluetooth sign while the camera is not linked (and whether it is nearby), its icon pulses while Bluetooth is being linked and turns green once it is; from the moment the camera's Wi-Fi is being joined it shows a Wi-Fi sign instead, pulsing until the Wi-Fi is up and green after, with the camera's battery. While Bluetooth is being linked, a × beside the chip cancels. It has a place of its own that is empty the rest of the time, and nothing else on the card moves or changes into another control as the connection changes, so a tap lands on what it was aimed at.

Tapping the chip, or the camera pill on the camera's pages, opens the camera sheet, which has a row each for Bluetooth and Wi-Fi saying what the link is doing, with Connect, Cancel or Disconnect. Connect on the Wi-Fi row joins the camera's Wi-Fi ahead of the Library. Disconnect on the Bluetooth row disconnects the camera altogether, its Wi-Fi included, and it stays disconnected until you connect it again or open one of its pages; Disconnect on the Wi-Fi row keeps Bluetooth. When connecting failed, the chip says so and the sheet says what to check, with Try again. Linking over Bluetooth never turns a camera on.

The power button turns the camera on or off over Bluetooth the way GR WORLD's does, linking it first when needed; a camera turned on with it stays on when GR Photo leaves it, and one turned off with it stays linked over Bluetooth. A camera that is off drops its Bluetooth link after a while by itself. GR Photo does not link it again and again then: the chip and the camera sheet say Camera off, GR Photo only listens for the camera saying it is on, and links it when it does. The power button still links it and turns it on.

## The camera's pages and its Wi-Fi

The card's Library, Shoot and Looks buttons open the camera's pages on that page; a floating tab bar at the bottom of the pages switches between them. The pages slide in over the Cameras screen.

Unless you join it from the camera sheet, the camera's Wi-Fi is joined only for the Library and for live view, and opening either turns the camera on first if it is off. While joining, the page lists two steps, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi; if joining fails, it says why and what to check, with Try again. Joining takes a while, so once the Wi-Fi is up it stays up when you move to a page that does not need it or go back to the Cameras screen, whose chip then shows the Wi-Fi in green: the Library and live view open without joining again, and a download carries on. Auto-disconnect (below) or Disconnect on the camera sheet's Wi-Fi row leaves the Wi-Fi, with Bluetooth still linked. Going back while the Wi-Fi is still being joined stops the join. A camera GR Photo turned on for a page is turned back off when you go back from the camera's pages or, if its Wi-Fi stayed up, when the Wi-Fi is left on the Cameras screen.

## When a link is lost

If the Wi-Fi is lost while it is in use (the Library or live view is open, or a download, movie copy or playback is running), the page stays and the app gets it back by itself, shown as reconnecting; a camera switched off at its power button shows a red "Camera off" pill and is turned back on over Bluetooth. If the Wi-Fi does not come back, the app returns to the Cameras screen with Bluetooth still linked and the chip saying so; if the camera is gone with its Bluetooth too without having said it was switching off (out of range, or its battery removed), it returns there with the chip saying the connection was lost. A camera that said it was switching off is not gone, even when its Bluetooth goes off with it: the page says Camera off, and GR Photo links it and joins its Wi-Fi again when it is switched back on. If the Wi-Fi is lost while nothing uses it, GR Photo lets it go without a word instead, and a camera switched off then stays off. On Android the loss is noticed as soon as the phone reports it; elsewhere, when the camera stops answering a Library request or its liveness polling. One camera is connected at a time.

A Bluetooth link that drops is linked again by itself, shown as reconnecting on the camera's pages and by the pulsing icon on the card's chip. While only Bluetooth linked the camera, also when the Library or live view was opened meanwhile and is waiting for that link, one that is not back after 40 seconds counts as gone (out of range, or its battery removed): the app returns to the Cameras screen, whose chip says the connection was lost. The link of a camera that is off is the exception: it is not linked again and does not count as gone, as described under The Cameras screen, and opening Shoot, Looks or the Library links it and turns it on. The saved looks on the home dock stay in front when that happens while they are shown.

## Shoot

Shoot opens in the mode used last, Remote the first time. Remote works over Bluetooth alone: it turns the camera on in shooting mode (or switches a camera in playback to shooting), shows the mode dial, shots left and battery, and has a large shutter, the self-timer (off, 2 s, 10 s) and the drive mode. A running self-timer turns the shutter into a countdown ring; tapping it cancels. On Android the volume buttons fire the shutter too.

Live view joins the camera's Wi-Fi and shows the camera's picture, dark and without the tab bar: tapping the picture focuses there, the aperture, shutter, ISO and EV readouts open a dial of the values the camera offers in its current mode, and the chips change the drive, self-timer, metering and Image Control. The picture is shown whole and as large as the window allows, on phones, foldables and tablets alike; the controls go above and below it, and lie over its edges where the window leaves them no room of their own. In a window as short as a phone on its side they go in a rail on each side of the picture, which stays in the middle of the screen: ×, the camera and the chips on one side, the readouts, the shutter and the last shot on the other, with the dial along the bottom of the picture; × is then the way back to Remote. Switching back to Remote (or ×) keeps the camera on and its Wi-Fi up, so live view starts at once the next time; it only stops a join that is still under way.

## Auto-disconnect

Auto-disconnect (Settings: 1, 5, 10 or 15 minutes, or Never; 5 by default) leaves the camera's Wi-Fi when GR Photo has not been used for that long, whichever screen is showing. With the Library or live view open it returns to the Cameras screen, the same way as disconnecting the Wi-Fi on the camera sheet; Shoot's Remote and Looks stay open on Bluetooth; and on the Cameras screen, whose chip says the Wi-Fi was left, a camera GR Photo turned on is turned back off. Touches, scrolls, key presses and moving between screens count as use, and so does a running download, movie copy, movie playback or live view. Time with the app in the background counts towards the limit. With Never, the Wi-Fi stays up until it is disconnected on the camera sheet, or the power button or switching cameras ends it.

## Joining the camera's Wi-Fi

On Android, the first Wi-Fi join of each camera is explained before Android's own network list asks you to approve it. Android shows that list again for an approved camera after its network was disconnected by hand in Wi-Fi settings. When that happens, a short message names the network to choose, and if the join fails, GR Photo explains why the list came back and offers Try again.

Joining is timed around the camera's access point, which comes up a varying time after its Wi-Fi is turned on. When the camera is off, GR Photo turns its Wi-Fi on right after waking it instead of first waiting 8 seconds to see whether the wake drops the Bluetooth link. If that early write fails, it waits for the link as before; if it goes through and the join is still looking for the network 8 seconds after the wake, GR Photo turns the Wi-Fi on once more (linking again first if the link dropped). On Android, Android looks for the network when asked and then only every 10-20 seconds, so for an approved camera GR Photo asks again every 7 seconds until Android starts connecting. It does not ask again while Android's network list is open, or on phones that join camera networks on a second Wi-Fi connection.

## Geotagging

GR cameras have no GPS. GR Photo can add where a photo was taken in two ways, which are turned on separately and can both be on: live geotagging, where the phone sends its location to the camera and the camera saves it in the photos it takes, and background geotagging, where the phone keeps a history of where it has been and GR Photo adds the nearest recorded place to a photo when it is downloaded. Live geotagging always wins: a location the camera saved is never replaced.

Live geotagging is turned on for each camera, with the Geotagging switch in the Location section of the camera's details, which ⋯ on the camera's card and the camera's row under Other cameras open. The section is not shown for a camera that cannot be sent a location. The first time a camera is linked after pairing (or, for a camera paired before GR Photo had geotagging, the first time it is linked), GR Photo offers it once, while none of the camera's pages is open: what is sent, that it is sent only while the camera is on, that it also keeps the camera's clock on the phone's time, and on Android that a notification shows while it runs, with Turn on and Not now. The offer comes before the phone's own question about location, and is not shown again whatever the answer; the switch in the camera's details shows the same explanation first if the camera was never offered it. If the phone refuses (location not allowed, only approximate location allowed, or location turned off for the whole phone), geotagging stays off and GR Photo says which it is, with a button to the page of the phone's settings where it is allowed.

While live geotagging is on and the camera is on and linked over Bluetooth, GR Photo follows the phone's location and writes it to the camera: as soon as there is a position, again whenever the phone has moved (by more than 5 metres or the position's own accuracy, and not more often than every 5 seconds), and a newly taken position at least every 3 minutes while standing still. A position older than a minute or less accurate than 100 metres is not sent. The camera forgets a location 10 minutes after it last got one, so a photo never gets a location from long ago. Nothing is sent to a camera that is off, and the phone is not located for a camera that is off or away.

The camera only takes a location while its own Store Location Info setting is on. Turning geotagging on in GR Photo turns Store Location Info on, and turning it off turns Store Location Info off, which also clears the location the camera holds; if the camera is off or away when the switch is flipped, this happens the next time it is on and linked. If Store Location Info is turned off in the camera's menu, GR Photo does not turn it back on by itself: the line under the switch says Store Location Info is off on the camera, with a Turn on button.

The line under the Geotagging switch says what it is doing: sending the phone's location, waiting for the phone's location, waiting for the camera to be switched on and in reach, or what is in the way. When the phone no longer allows the location (the permission was taken away, only approximate location is allowed, or location is off for the phone), geotagging stays on, the line says which it is, and a button opens the phone's settings; it carries on by itself once it is allowed again.

Live geotagging carries on while GR Photo is not on screen. On Android a notification shows while it does, named after the camera, with what it is doing and a Stop button. Stop ends it until Resume is pressed in the camera's details or the camera is next switched on. When the camera has not been on for an hour the notification goes away, and geotagging waits for the camera to be switched on again. On Android, closing GR Photo's window while live geotagging runs closes the camera's pages and leaves the camera's Wi-Fi, as Disconnect on the Wi-Fi row does (a download still running keeps the Wi-Fi until it is done); Bluetooth and geotagging carry on.

By itself, live geotagging starts when the camera is switched on while GR Photo is open. Where the phone can start GR Photo for a camera, the camera's details have an Automatic start row under the Geotagging switch while geotagging is on, which says so and offers Set up. Set up asks for location to be allowed all the time and, on Android, for the camera to be linked to the phone in a system prompt. Once both are in place the row says that geotagging starts whenever the camera is switched on, also when GR Photo is closed: the phone notices the camera being switched on, starts GR Photo without showing it, and GR Photo links the camera and starts sending. If the phone will not let GR Photo use the location from there, the line under the switch says that geotagging starts when GR Photo is open, and it starts when GR Photo is next opened. If setting it up is refused, the row says what is missing, with the phone's settings for the location or Set up again for the link to the camera.

With several cameras paired, live geotagging runs for one camera at a time. A camera with geotagging on that is switched on becomes the current camera and is connected, unless another camera is on and linked at that moment; then it is left alone until that one is off or disconnected.

Sync camera clock, in the Location section of the camera's details and on by default, keeps the camera's clock on the phone's time: when the camera is linked while it is on and every hour after that, GR Photo reads the camera's clock and sets it if it is 2 seconds or more off. While geotagging is on for the camera the clock is kept in sync whatever the switch says, and the switch is shown on and cannot be changed, with a line saying why. A GR IV clears its location when its clock is changed, so GR Photo sends a newly taken position right after setting the clock.

Background geotagging is a switch in Settings, under Location. Turning it on first explains the two ways and what each costs: live geotagging is accurate, is written by the camera, and needs the camera linked over Bluetooth and switched on; background geotagging needs nothing while shooting, and is less accurate because the phone records a place only now and then. Only then does the phone ask for location all the time, which background geotagging needs; without it the switch stays off and Settings says what is missing, with a button to the phone's settings.

While it is on, the phone records where it is from time to time, also while GR Photo is closed, and GR Photo keeps those places for 90 days. The history stays on the phone. When a JPEG or DNG is downloaded and has no location yet, GR Photo writes into the downloaded file the place recorded closest in time to when the photo was taken, before or after; a photo taken before the first recorded place gets that place only if it was recorded within 10 minutes of the photo. The time is the one the camera wrote into the photo, which is one reason the camera's clock is kept in sync. Photos downloaded more than 90 days after they were taken get no location from background geotagging, movies get none, and the photo on the camera's card is not changed. While live geotagging runs with background geotagging on, the positions it takes are kept in the history too.

If location all the time is taken away later, background geotagging stays on and shows as Paused, with what is missing and a button to the phone's settings, and resumes by itself when it is allowed again. Turning it off stops the recording; what was recorded stays until it is 90 days old or deleted. Delete location history, in the same section, asks first and then deletes every place recorded so far, including those the phone has not handed to GR Photo yet. Photos downloaded afterwards cannot get one of those places, and photos already downloaded keep theirs. Deleting does not turn background geotagging off: the phone goes on recording, so a photo taken shortly before the deletion can still get the next place recorded, if that is within 10 minutes of the photo.
